# ROBOT NEKO

jacob, 2026-09-10, on the first sketch: *"hey thats funny, and honestly? i like this. thats
episode of robot neko"* — and then: *"this should be the kind of cartoon that gets better
everytime, as you are developing your skill and tools."*

So every episode has two deliverables: **the episode**, and **a better pipeline** — a new
engine capability, a new tool, or a new line in the ledger below. An episode that teaches
nothing has only done half the job.

The show's look is `design/stop_motion.md` (bodies exposed on twos, lens smooth, puppet
jaws, the animator's hand). The engine's run skill is `run-reva`; film grammar is
`film-craft`; missing engine features are `build-the-capability`. **Load all three.**

## THE MODEL: ROBOT CHICKEN

jacob, 2026-09-11: *"remember robot neko is inspired by robot chicken"* — said while the
director was treating two things as blockers that the model simply settles.

Robot Chicken is stop-motion **action figures**: crude, fast, hard cuts, punchline and OUT.

⛔ **AND IT IS NOT A LICENCE FOR BAD ANIMATION.** jacob, 2026-09-12, when he saw how this
was written down: *"when i said robot chicken inspired i never meant to reduce the animation
quality. more so that it was ok to have cheap mouths on painted faces and to be funny, like
robot chicken"* — and separately, *"i do like a stop motion look though"*. So:

| on model | NOT on model |
|---|---|
| a **cheap mouth** — a hinge jaw on raw loudness, no visemes | a face that does not work **at all** |
| a painted low-poly face that cannot emote | a rig whose morphs are present but unreachable |
| the **stepped exposure clock** — he likes the look, and it is a TOOL: *"stop motion is a tool for us to use not an absolute creqtive restriction"* (ep 12). Choose it per shot, per episode, for what it gives | bodies standing in a line not doing anything |
| crude props, hard cuts, no polish for its own sake | staging nobody bothered to compose |

- **A RIGID FACE IS ON MODEL — A BROKEN ONE IS NOT.** A body with no jaw bone and a painted
  face can still SPEAK; that is an action figure (ep 6: Mrs. Parsons fails `face_marks` with
  `no mouth band` on every copy on both drives, and talks anyway). ⛔ But this sentence is
  exactly what a future session would reach for to excuse a REGRESSION, and one already
  happened: kaeli's 92 morphs were present and unreachable through the whole of ep 7 and it
  shipped. "It's Robot Chicken" does not cover that. Cheapness is a choice; brokenness is a bug.
- **THE BODIES HAVE TO DO SOMETHING.** jacob, same day: *"reva and kaeli shouldn't be just
  staring at each other the whole time. let them walk around, or do things idk. or interact
  with each other or objects."* Two puppets pointed at each other for ninety seconds is not
  the style, it is the absence of staging. Give them marks to walk to and objects to handle —
  ep 8 makes someone physically pick a television up off the grass.
- **LENGTH IS NOT A CAP — IT IS A SYMPTOM TO READ.** Measured on the delivered files: ep 1
  **58.8s**, ep 2 **63.3s**, ep 3 **80.5s**, ep 4 **77.8s**, ep 5 **95.6s** — **+63% in five
  episodes**. jacob, when shown that: *"longer is ok as long as it is quality. i dont want to
  constraign your creativity. having limitations like this could help with quality too.. ik"*
  So there is no target runtime and no cap. The test is per BEAT, not per minute: **does this
  beat earn its place, or is the episode just enjoying itself?** Cut what repeats (ep 6 ran its
  polygraph three times — two is the joke, three is a procedure), keep what lands, and let the
  button END the episode rather than set up tomorrow. A long episode where every beat earns is
  fine; a 60s one padded with victory laps is not. Use the drift as a prompt to re-read the
  script, never as a reason to trim something good.

## The house rule: every gag is a staging key

A joke the frame can SHOW beats one the audience is told, and the engine only shows what a
script line can ask for. When a gag needs something no key can express, that is a
capability to build (ep 1 built `step=`, ep 2 built `tod=`/`snap=`/`tone=`/slot 3).

## The pipeline

```bash
PY=/c/Users/jacob/AppData/Local/Python/pythoncore-3.14-64/python.exe
D=.claude/skills/run-reva/driver.py
N=D:/reva902/robot_neko
```

1. **Premise + script** → `reva_native/scenes/<name>.txt`. Header says the joke, the keys it
   uses, and which ledger lessons it applies. Run `python skate_src/film/shotgrammar.py`.
2. **Voices: KOKORO, never Piper.** (jacob: *"the tts is pretty bad … swithc to kokoro"*.)
   `$PY reva_native/scenes/say_kokoro.py <name>.txt` then
   `$PY reva_native/scenes/compile_scene.py reva_native/scenes/<name>.txt` — read the line
   times it prints; `sfx` and set `cue`s are placed against them.
3. **Foley** the shelf lacks: `$PY skate_src/robot_neko/foley.py clap` (add makers there).
4. **The set** → `reva_native/sets/<name>.set`. Shoot an OVERHEAD before placing anything:
   `$PY $D lab $N/map.png --new --frame 480 --ctl "face=0 grid=0 dqs=0 set=<name> cam=X,48,Z-0.5,X,0,Z,60"`
   (image up = +z, image right = −x, ~13 px per unit at that height). Mark and paths go
   where the map says, not where a guess says.
5. **Bench anything new before casting it**: `$PY $D bench $N/b.png --subject <body> --clip <clip> --orbit 4 --cols 3`.
6. **8fps test take** — cheap, ~5 min:
   `$PY $D scene $N/<name>_t8.mp4 --name <name> --seconds <len+2> --fps 8 --new --raw ...`
   then `$PY skate_src/robot_neko/scenesheet.py $N/<name>_t8.mp4 reva_native/scenes/<name>.scn $N/sheet.png --fps 8`
   and pull FULL frames of the worst-looking tiles. Read the `.log` beside the mp4
   (`[stage] … NOT FOUND`, `[headroom]`, `[step]`, `[tod]`, `[snap]`, `[cue]`).
7. **Fix, re-test** until the sheet is right. Only then:
8. **60fps take** — jacob, 2026-09-12: *"we need to be filming at 60fps, not 24"*. Same
   command, `--fps 60`. At `step=12` that is ONE exposure and FOUR holds rather than 24fps's
   alternating pair, so the bodies still step on twelves while the LENS gets genuinely smooth
   motion — which is the look the mode was always after (`stop_motion.md`: the camera stays
   smooth, only the puppets step). Costs ~2.5x the render of a 24fps take; budget for it.
   ⛔ At 60, `mp4steps.py`'s LETTERS stop meaning anything. A frame reads `h` only when BOTH of
   its neighbours move more than twice as much, so the middle of a four-frame hold reads `E`. Read
   the NUMBERS instead: one change, then four ~0.00. A clean ep 9 window reads `0.00 0.00 0.00 0.00
   0.76 0.00 0.00 0.00 0.00 0.33 0.00`. Two large changes in a row on a travelling body is the
   double step (ep 9, `locoPublish`).
9. **Package**: `$PY skate_src/robot_neko/package.py --ep N --title TITLE --in raw.mp4 --out $N/epNN_title.mp4`
   — it keeps the TAKE'S frame rate and prints it (`packaging at 60 fps`; `--fps` overrides). LOOK
   at the `_card.png` it writes.
10. **Verify the DELIVERED file** — the packaged mp4, never the take. ⛔ Ep 8's checks ran on its
    60fps take while the file jacob got was 24 (ep 9 ledger). `ffprobe` the file's `r_frame_rate`
    and compare it with the take's. Then run `$PY skate_src/robot_neko/verify.py $N/epNN_title.mp4 --steps … --clip … --frames …`
    with every scene time shifted by the card (~2.8 s), and check the audio under any `sfx`. LOOK at
    the montage. Deliver with SendUserFile. A file over 30 MiB reaches only the desktop app, not
    jacob's phone.
11. **Update the ledger below** with what this episode taught.
